# Phase 0: Data (plain-language summary)

1. `scripts/prepare_data.py` reads Portuguese and English Wikipedia from Hugging Face in *row groups* (blocks of ~1000 consecutive articles) visited in a seeded random order, so the sample spans the whole dump; only the blocks used are downloaded (cached in `data/raw/rowgroups/`). See `results/deviations.md` D1.
2. Each article title is hashed twice with SHA-1. The first hash keeps ~1 in 5 articles; the second puts the article in train / val / test / relearn_fresh. Because the split depends only on the title, a whole article always lands in one split, so no text leaks from train into val or test.
3. Cleaning (`clean_text` in `src/unlearnlang/data.py`): Unicode NFC, lowercase, curly quotes and dashes mapped to their plain ASCII versions, all whitespace collapsed to one space, and any other character replaced by UNK (`□`). Articles with more than 0.5% UNK are dropped.
4. Purity filter: count the share of words that are English stopwords and Portuguese stopwords. A Portuguese article is dropped if more than 5% of its words are English stopwords, or if English stopwords outnumber Portuguese ones. An English article is dropped only if Portuguese stopwords outnumber English ones: a 5% rule there mostly removed English articles about Brazilian places, whose names contain "de", "do" and "da" (deviation D3). Only the Portuguese side has to be pure.
5. The vocabulary has 62 symbols: 60 allowed characters, UNK and EOS. EOS (`⏎` when printed) separates articles. Each split is saved as one long `uint8` file, `processed/{split}.bin`, with one byte per character.
6. `BatchSampler` takes random 513-character windows: the first 512 characters are the input and the same window shifted by one is the target. It has its own seeded generator, so batches can be reproduced.
7. Word lists for the leakage metric: `EN_only` holds words that are common in English (≥ 2 per million) and at least 50× rarer in Portuguese. `PT_only` is the mirror. `REAL` holds every word seen at least 0.5 times per million in either corpus.
8. Tests (`tests/test_data.py`) check that no title appears in two splits, that every title hashes to its own split, that relearn_fresh does not overlap the forget set (by title and by text), that encode followed by decode gives the text back, the UNK rate, and the EOS count.
9. Why random row groups: reading the dump from its start would favour the oldest, longest, best-known articles (first try: ~20k chars/article; now ~2.5k), and EN would come from a far smaller slice of its dump than PT. Remaining limitation: articles inside one block are related in time (cluster sampling).
10. Known limitation: Portuguese articles still contain some English, mostly in bibliographies and in song or book titles. In pt_train, "the" occurs 1,440 times per million words but "was" only 8, so the English is in names and titles, not sentences. θ_ref has seen *close to* no English, not none.
